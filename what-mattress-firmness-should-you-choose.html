Mattress Buying Guide

What Mattress Firmness Should You Choose?

Mattress firmness affects how supported and comfortable your bed feels. The right choice depends on your sleeping position, body support needs and the type of sleeping surface you personally prefer.

Practical UK buying guide
Quick answer

Side sleepers often prefer a softer surface that allows the shoulders and hips to settle comfortably. Medium firmness is a versatile option for many sleepers, while firmer mattresses can suit people who prefer stronger support.

What does mattress firmness actually mean?

Firmness describes how the mattress feels when you lie on it. It is different from the overall quality or support of a mattress. A mattress can feel soft while still providing useful support underneath the sleeping surface.

Your preferred firmness is therefore a personal decision rather than something that can be determined by one universal rule.

Which firmness suits different sleeping positions?

Sleeping position Often suitable Why
Side sleeping Soft to medium Allows more cushioning around shoulders and hips.
Back sleeping Medium to firm Can provide a balanced combination of comfort and support.
Front sleeping Medium to firm May provide a more supportive sleeping surface.
Combination sleeping Medium A versatile middle ground for changing positions.

Why side sleepers often prefer softer mattresses

When sleeping on your side, pressure can build around the shoulders and hips. A slightly softer surface can allow these areas to settle into the mattress rather than feeling as though you are lying directly on top of it.

However, going too soft can reduce the feeling of stability, so the underlying support remains important.

When might a firmer mattress be preferable?

Some people prefer the more stable feeling of a firmer sleeping surface, particularly when sleeping on their back or front.

A firmer mattress can also appeal to sleepers who dislike the sinking sensation associated with softer mattress constructions.

Think about your current mattress

  • Do you feel pressure around your shoulders or hips?
  • Do you feel that your current mattress allows too much sinking?
  • Do you prefer a stable surface or a more cushioned feel?
  • Do you regularly change sleeping positions?

Do not confuse firmness with support

Firmness is the initial feel of the mattress. Support relates to how effectively the mattress holds your body in a comfortable position.

This is why a softer mattress is not automatically less supportive, and a firm mattress is not automatically better.

What if you are unsure about firmness?

If you regularly move between sleeping positions, a medium-feeling mattress can be a useful starting point because it avoids going too far towards either extreme.

You should also consider the construction of the mattress, as pocket springs, memory foam and pillow top layers can all change the way firmness is experienced.

Final firmness checklist

  • Identify your main sleeping position.
  • Consider whether you prefer cushioning or a stable surface.
  • Think about pressure around shoulders and hips.
  • Separate firmness from overall support.
  • Compare the mattress construction, not just the firmness label.
  • Choose the correct mattress size for your bed.

What Size Bed Should You Choose for a Child?

Choosing a child's bed size is about more than the mattress. Bedroom space, growing room, furniture placement and everyday practicality all need to be considered before buying.

Quick answer For many children's bedrooms, a 3ft Single provides a practical balance between sleeping space and room around the bed. A larger option can make sense when the bedroom has enough space and you want the bed to remain useful for longer.

The right bed size can make a noticeable difference to how comfortable and practical a child's bedroom feels. A bed that is too large can dominate a small room, while a very small sleeping surface may not provide enough useful space as the child grows.

Before choosing a size, consider the room itself, the child's current needs and how you expect the bedroom to be used over the next few years.

What is the most common starting point?

A 3ft Single is often a practical starting point for a child's bedroom because it provides a full single sleeping surface without taking up the floor area of a larger double-size bed.

However, the right choice depends on the room. A child's bedroom should still have enough space for wardrobes, a desk, storage and a comfortable walkway.

How much room does a child actually need?

The mattress is only part of the calculation. You also need to allow for the bed frame, headboard and space around the bed.

Measure the complete area that the bed will occupy, then mark out where other furniture will sit. This makes it easier to see whether the chosen size leaves enough room for everyday movement.

Should you choose a bed with room to grow?

Buying a size that can remain useful as a child grows can reduce the need to replace the bed quickly. However, this only makes sense if the larger size works with the room.

Think about the bedroom as a whole rather than choosing a larger bed only because it offers more sleeping space.

Measure before ordering

  1. Measure the width of the available wall space.
  2. Measure the length available from the head of the bed.
  3. Allow room for walking around the bed.
  4. Check wardrobe and drawer opening areas.
  5. Consider where a desk or other furniture will be positioned.
  6. Check the bed's full external dimensions, not just the mattress size.

What size mattress will you need?

The mattress must match the bed size and the specific frame. Do not assume that two beds described using the same size will necessarily have exactly the same external dimensions.

Always check the manufacturer's listed mattress dimensions and the overall dimensions of the bed before ordering.

What if the bedroom is very small?

In a compact bedroom, the smallest suitable sleeping size can often be the better choice because it leaves more usable space for other furniture and everyday movement.

Look carefully at the bed's external width and length. A few extra inches can affect whether a wardrobe door opens comfortably or whether there is enough space beside the bed.

Should style affect the size decision?

Style should come after practicality. A large upholstered bed may look impressive, but if it leaves very little usable space around it, it may not be the best choice for the room.

Choose the appropriate size first, then consider upholstery, colour, headboard design and other visual details.

Final thoughts

Choosing the right bed size for a child is mainly about balancing sleeping space with the amount of usable bedroom space left around the bed.

Measure the room carefully, consider how the child may use the bedroom as they grow and check the complete bed dimensions before making a decision.

How to Choose a Kids Bed for a Small Bedroom

A small child's bedroom needs careful planning. The right bed can provide a comfortable sleeping space without leaving the room feeling cramped.

Quick answer Start with the room's actual dimensions, then choose the smallest bed size that comfortably meets the child's needs. Pay close attention to the bed's external dimensions, surrounding furniture and available walking space.

Small bedrooms can be difficult to arrange because the bed is usually the largest item in the room. Once the bed, wardrobe and other furniture are positioned, there may be very little floor space left.

That makes the bed's overall footprint particularly important. Looking only at the mattress size can give you an inaccurate idea of how much room the finished bed will actually occupy.

Start by measuring the room

Before looking at different designs, measure the bedroom from wall to wall. Note the position of doors, windows, radiators, wardrobes and any fixed furniture.

Then consider where the bed could realistically sit. A simple floor plan or masking-tape outline can help you visualise the available space before buying.

Which bed size works best in a small room?

A 3ft Single can be a practical choice because it provides a standard single sleeping surface without the footprint of a larger double-size bed.

If the bedroom is particularly narrow, the external width of the frame can be just as important as the mattress width. Upholstery and headboards may add additional dimensions.

Should the bed go against a wall?

Positioning a bed against a wall can sometimes make a small bedroom easier to arrange, particularly when floor space is limited.

However, consider how the child will get into and out of bed and whether the final arrangement makes it difficult to change bedding or clean around the frame.

Think vertically as well as horizontally

When floor space is limited, the visual height of the bed can also affect how spacious the room feels.

A very tall headboard can become a strong visual feature, while a simpler design may make the room feel more open. This does not mean a tall headboard is unsuitable, but it should be considered alongside the size and layout of the room.

What about storage?

Storage is often one of the biggest challenges in a small bedroom. If the chosen bed includes a storage option, check how it opens and whether the room provides enough clearance to use it.

Storage should not be added simply because it sounds useful. The mechanism needs to remain practical once the bed and other furniture are installed.

How to avoid making the room feel crowded

  • Choose furniture with a clear and simple footprint.
  • Avoid oversized bedside furniture where possible.
  • Keep the main walking route clear.
  • Use the bed as the main visual anchor rather than overcrowding the room.
  • Consider lighter upholstery colours if you want a more open visual feel.
  • Measure every large item before committing to the final layout.

Measure around the bed, not just underneath it

A common mistake is to measure an empty rectangle and assume the bed will fit. In practice, the surrounding space matters just as much.

  1. Measure the proposed bed position.
  2. Mark the external bed footprint.
  3. Place the wardrobe and other furniture on the same plan.
  4. Check door and drawer opening areas.
  5. Walk through the remaining space and assess whether it feels practical.

Final thoughts

Choosing a kids bed for a small bedroom is mainly about finding the right balance between sleeping space and usable floor area.

Measure the complete bed dimensions, think carefully about furniture placement and avoid choosing a size that leaves the room difficult to use.

Which Mattress Type Is Best for Your Sleeping Position?

Your sleeping position can influence which mattress construction feels most comfortable. Memory foam, pocket sprung and pillow top designs all have different characteristics, so it helps to compare them before buying.

Practical UK buying guide
Quick answer

Side sleepers may prefer cushioning from memory foam or a pillow top, while back and front sleepers may prefer the more structured feel of pocket sprung or supportive mattress designs. Combination sleepers often benefit from a balanced construction.

Why your sleeping position matters

Your body interacts with a mattress differently depending on how you sleep. A side sleeper places more pressure through the shoulders and hips, while a back sleeper distributes weight differently across the sleeping surface.

This is why choosing a mattress purely because it sounds luxurious or has a particular spring count is not always the best approach.

Memory foam mattresses

Memory foam is known for its contouring feel. The foam can respond to body weight and create a more cushioned sensation around areas that press into the mattress.

This can make memory foam appealing to side sleepers and people who enjoy a more moulded sleeping surface.

Pocket sprung mattresses

Pocket springs use individually contained springs that can respond more independently across the sleeping surface. They can provide a structured and supportive feel while retaining some responsiveness.

Pocket sprung designs can be particularly appealing if you prefer a mattress that feels supportive without the deeper contouring sensation associated with some foams.

Pillow top mattresses

A pillow top adds an additional cushioned layer to the upper surface of the mattress. This can create a softer and more luxurious initial feel while the construction beneath provides support.

Mattress type Typical feel May suit
Memory foam Contouring and cushioned Side sleepers and people who like a moulded feel
Pocket sprung Structured and responsive Sleepers wanting spring-based support
Pillow top Soft and cushioned on top People who prefer a more luxurious surface

What about combination sleepers?

If you move between your side, back and front during the night, you may not want a mattress designed around one sleeping position alone.

A balanced construction can provide enough cushioning for side sleeping while still feeling stable when you move onto your back.

Match the mattress to your preference

  • Choose memory foam if you like a contouring feel.
  • Consider pocket springs if you prefer responsive support.
  • Consider a pillow top if you want additional surface cushioning.
  • Look at the complete construction rather than one feature alone.

Do not choose by sleeping position alone

Sleeping position is useful, but comfort preference matters too. Two people who both sleep on their sides may prefer very different mattress feels.

Think about your current mattress. If you enjoy its cushioning but want better support, look for a construction that addresses both requirements rather than simply switching to the opposite firmness.

Final mattress comparison checklist

  • Identify your main sleeping position.
  • Decide whether you prefer cushioning or responsiveness.
  • Compare memory foam and pocket spring construction.
  • Consider whether a pillow top would improve surface comfort.
  • Check mattress size before ordering.
  • Make sure the mattress is suitable for your bed base.
